01 · Vox Amelior

Forget nothing. Share nothing.

The story · home life

A memory that never leaves your pocket.

Most of what matters is said out loud: who’s coming on Thursday, what the doctor said, the thing a friend asked you to remember. Then it’s gone. Vox Amelior keeps it.

It listens on your phone, and only there. It writes down what’s said around you, learns whose voice is whose after you name it once, and files every line by day and by conversation. Later you ask in plain words, “What did Jordan say I shouldn’t forget?”, and it answers from what was actually said, with the time and the place.

Then it turns the question around. Ask about yourself: who you thanked this week, how much you talked and how much you listened. The voice you hear most is yours.

Nothing is uploaded. No account, no subscription, no server to trust. A memory this personal should be the one thing that never leaves your pocket, and with small models doing the work on the phone, it doesn’t have to.

What
A private memory for your conversations: it listens, writes down who said what, and answers questions about your day.
Why
Small models on your phone do the work you’d otherwise rent a server for. No subscription, no upload, no waiting on a signal that isn’t there.
Frontier
Every model is a swappable part. When a better open model ships, it drops in, and the whole memory gets sharper.

The architecture

One phone. One work queue.

Everything heavy runs in one background service with a single work queue, so speech recognition and the language model never fight over the phone’s memory.

  1. 01CaptureAn always-on service holds the microphone. A speech detector keeps only voice, with adjustable boost, sensitivity and sentence pause.Silero VAD
  2. 02Queue on diskSpeech is written to disk before anything else happens, so it survives a restart. A chunk that fails is retried once, then set aside instead of lost.
  3. 03One job at a timeA single scheduler decides what runs: a question you’re waiting on first, then transcription, then background reviews in small steps.
  4. 04Transcribe and attributeEach sentence is written down with punctuation, split by speaker, matched to a voiceprint and tagged with its tone of voice.Parakeet TDT 0.6BSortformerTitaNetSenseVoice
  5. 05Store and indexLines land in SQLite with full-text search, grouped into days and conversations. EmbeddingGemma adds compact vectors for search by meaning.SQLite FTS5EmbeddingGemma
  6. 06Answer and actGemma 4 reads the lines search picks and can use tools: search conversations, read a week’s timeline, save notes, set reminders.Gemma 4LiteRT-LM
  7. 07In your glassesThe answer, or just the name you can’t place, lands in the lenses for the moment you need it.Even Realities G1

Measured, not guessed. “Test this phone” fills Gemma’s context from 2,048 up to 32,768 tokens and checks it can still repeat a code word; prompts and review steps are sized from the result.

Search tuned on real scores. Related lines scored 0.44 to 0.62 and unrelated ones up to 0.43, so no fixed cut-off could separate them. Search keeps lines close to the best match instead.

Tested like a product. 400+ automated tests, plus a CI tour that installs the release build, plays synthetic voices into it and drives every screen with the real models.

How it works

  1. It listens, on your phone and only there.
  2. It writes each line down and learns whose voice it is.
  3. You ask in plain words: “What did Jordan say I shouldn’t forget?”
  4. The answer comes back, or the name lands in your glasses.

AI, spent wisely

One scheduler runs one job at a time, and a question you’re waiting on goes first. That’s how a phone does a data centre’s work: by never doing two things badly when it can do one thing well. It even works in airplane mode.

  • 400+ automated tests
  • No cloud AI, no analytics, out of cloud backups
  • People and conversations shown are examples

02 · Even G1

Your computer, one look away.

The story · work life

Check in. Don’t sit in.

Building software used to mean sitting at a desk and watching it happen. Now frontier AI does the typing, and most of the work is waiting. So why sit there?

Vox puts a real terminal in the Even Realities G1 glasses: your computer at home, your laptop at work over SSH, or a whole Linux system on the phone itself. Tell Claude what to build, then go and make dinner. The lenses stay dark until you look up.

Glance up and the screen fades in: the step Claude is on, a diff, the test results. Look down and it’s gone. When it needs you, a heads-up says so, and nothing else interrupts.

Getting there took the glasses apart in software. Out of the box they show a few lines of text; I built on reverse-engineered firmware and optimized it for this: a 16-grey video mode, both arms drawing in step, and a display that fades with the tilt of your head.

What
A second screen for any computer: your terminal or whole desktop, drawn for the lenses and sent over Bluetooth.
Why
Hand the work to frontier AI and walk away. You look up for progress, and for the questions only you can answer.
Frontier
The builder is frontier AI. When the models get better, every build does, with nothing for me to rewrite.

The architecture

The phone thinks. The glasses draw.

Vox runs every model on the phone and sends the glasses only what to draw.

  1. 01SourcesA terminal on the phone or a computer over SSH, kept alive by mosh as the phone changes networks; the phone’s screen; a laptop’s screen; the glasses’ own microphone.moshJSchMediaProjectionLC3
  2. 02One microphoneOne shared microphone feeds every feature. A speech detector keeps only voice, with a second, keener pass for soft voices.Silero VAD
  3. 03UnderstandEach phrase is written down with word times, matched to a voiceprint, split into turns and tagged with its tone, on the phone.Parakeet TDT 0.6BWeSpeakerpyannoteSenseVoice
  4. 04Think, taking turnsA small model answers on the phone through llama.cpp; while it thinks, transcription waits and catches up after. Frontier AI works in the phone’s own terminal.llama.cppLFM2.5Frontier AI
  5. 05Draw for the lensTerminal cells, screen frames and panels are drawn for a 640 × 480 display per eye and sent to both arms at once.Bluetooth LE16-grey video
  6. 06On the glassesThe firmware runs small programs on each arm: the terminal fades in as you look up and out as you look down, and both eyes keep one brightness.Firmware VM

Both eyes agree. Every frame goes to both arms at once over Bluetooth LE, so the picture never tears between your eyes.

The network can drop; the session can’t. mosh holds the terminal as the phone moves from Wi-Fi to cellular and back.

No computer needed. Termux runs a full Linux on the phone, so there’s always a terminal to look up at.

How it works

  1. Pick a computer: the one at home, the one at work, or just the phone.
  2. Say what to build; Parakeet types it into the prompt.
  3. Walk away. Heads-up shows only progress and the notifications that matter.
  4. Look up to check in; look down to carry on.

Building, without the chair

Building used to mean sitting. Now the work keeps moving while you do, and the glasses tap you only when it counts. The machine does the typing; you do the deciding.

  • 640 × 480 per eye, up to 30 frames a second
  • A terminal, a phone’s screen or a laptop’s
  • Speak into the glasses; Parakeet types it into any prompt

03 · The podcast

The employee handbook, now with a plot.

The story · work life, brain life

One AI does the work. The other tells the story.

The first week of a new job is a fire hose: handbooks, policies, how the place really runs. Reading it at a desk is the slowest way to learn it.

Name a subject, the company you just joined, a book, a stretch of history, and Claude writes it as a show for two hosts: where it came from, the rules it lives by, precisely how it all works, pitched at your level.

Your phone reads it with natural voices, on the device, so it plays in the car, on a walk, or while Claude builds something in your glasses. Two voices make it a conversation instead of a lecture, and conversations stick.

It began as Narrator Studio, an app that turns any text into a chaptered podcast. Now it lives in Vox, one tap from everything else.

What
A podcast about anything you name, written by Claude and voiced on your phone by two hosts.
Why
It turns dead time into the good kind: a commute, a chore or a long build becomes an episode about the thing you most need to know.
Frontier
Claude writes every script. As the writer improves, so does the show, episode after episode.

The architecture

From subject to episode.

Text goes through a plan, a cast of voices and a mixer, then plays live or exports with chapters. The voices run in a separate worker, so the app stays responsive while they speak.

  1. 01Your subjectA topic, a book, a company: from what you give it or what’s public.
  2. 02ScriptClaude writes the episode at your level; text, Markdown or PDF is cleaned and split into sentences.Claude
  3. 03PlanStyle, speakers, pauses, sound and music cues.
  4. 04VoicesOn-device engines in a worker isolate; two hosts in Kokoro’s natural voices.Kokoro
  5. 05MixOriginal music ducked under the speech, at 48 kHz.
  6. 06Play or exportA read-along player, or an M4A with chapters. Vox plays it in the car, on a walk, or while Claude builds.M4A

A model ported by hand. VibeVoice had no phone runtime, so CI converts it to ONNX and I rewrote its inference loop and tokenizer in Dart, checked against the Python reference before it shipped.

Quality checked on every build. CI renders a sample episode with the real voices and Whisper transcribes it: a 2.6% word error rate.

Ears over metrics. Automatic scores ranked some robotic voices highest. After listening tests, the characters went back on the more natural voices.

How it works

  1. Type a subject, or tap an idea.
  2. Claude writes a two-host episode at your level.
  3. Kokoro voices it, on the phone.
  4. Press play, wherever you are.

Leisure that pays you back

New job? Make it a series. The history is the plot, the rules are the twists, and how it all works is the finale. By Monday you know the place better than the people who wrote the handbook.

  • Written from what you give it, or what’s public
  • Each episode builds on what you already know
  • Northwind is an example company

04 · Vox Learn

Don’t get it? Ask, right then and there.

The story · brain life

A teacher with endless patience and no office hours.

Learning stalls at one moment: you don’t get something, and there’s no one to ask. Vox Learn is built around that moment.

Pick a subject. Claude writes the lessons at your level and Kokoro reads them aloud, so a commute becomes a class. Don’t follow? Ask, right then and there, and the answer comes in your ear.

On the drive home it quizzes you, and you answer out loud. Parakeet hears you, the phone judges the answer offline, and points, levels and a streak turn it into a game you want to keep playing.

Then it does the part most courses skip: the questions you missed come back after 1, 3, 7, 16 and 35 days, just before you’d forget them.

What
Whole subjects as short lessons, then a spoken quiz with points, streaks and spaced review.
Why
Recall beats rereading. Answering out loud makes it stick, and the drive you already make becomes the class you never had time for.
Frontier
Claude writes the lessons, so a better model means a better teacher, overnight.

The architecture

Lessons in. Answers out loud.

Claude writes the lessons; everything after that, from the voice to the grading to the schedule, runs on the phone.

  1. 01LessonClaude writes it at your level; Kokoro reads it aloud.ClaudeKokoro
  2. 02AskStuck? Ask out loud, mid-lesson, and get the answer in your ear.
  3. 03ListenNVIDIA Parakeet hears your answer, on the phone.Parakeet
  4. 04JudgeAnswers are judged on the phone, offline: points, a level, a streak.
  5. 05ReviewMissed questions come back after 1, 3, 7, 16 and 35 days.spaced review
  6. 06The glassesDark in the car; the questions and the answers come in your ear.Even Realities G1

Hands on the wheel. In the car it’s all voice, and the glasses stay dark.

Grading without a signal. Answers are judged on the phone, so a tunnel never costs you a streak.

Memory science, done for you. Spaced review brings each miss back on a widening schedule, so it sticks.

How it works

  1. Pick a subject, any subject.
  2. Listen to a lesson pitched at your level.
  3. Lost? Ask out loud; the answer comes in your ear.
  4. Six questions after each episode, answered by voice.

Curiosity, on your schedule

No syllabus but yours, no pace but yours, no question too small to ask twice. It’s the patient tutor most people never get, riding along in the passenger seat.

  • Six questions after each episode
  • Answers judged on the phone, offline
  • The quiz shown is an example

The book that updates itself.

Most things you build start ageing the day they ship. These start improving.

Each card is a part, not a pillar. Vox runs open models that drop out the day a better one ships; the building, the scripts and the lessons are Claude’s, and every new Claude is a better engineer, writer and teacher on day one.
